decisions, and came back approved in one word — every item held, both decisions confirmed, and not one
byte of `cats-vs-mechs.html` changed.

conditional on what the checkpoint returned.

developer — all thirteen operated items (A1–A7 the feature itself, B8–B13 the things no test in this

Per the plan's own `<resume-signal>` — *"Type 'approved' if everything held and both decisions are
confirmed"* — that resolves as: every item 1–13 held, decision 14 confirmed, decision 15 confirmed.

**Task 2** therefore took the branch the plan wrote for exactly this outcome: *"If the developer
confirmed both decisions and every item held, change no code. Record the rehearsal and finish."*
No code was changed. This SUMMARY is the deliverable.

## The Two Decisions

### 14. The damage-key scope for the projection — **CONFIRMED, health-only**

The developer confirmed that the projection counts **health only**. An action that strips shield is
**NAMED** in the admission line rather than counted.

The two figures the decision was made on, measured by plan 03.1-02's probe G with a cats action `Rend`
(cost 1 action point, target shield −2) authored onto the board:

| `DAMAGE_KEYS` | cats read | admitted |
|---|---|---|
| `['hp']` (shipped, **confirmed**) | `{ perTurn: 3, hit: 1, ehp: 27, soak: 27, fast: 9, slow: 9 }` — **≈9 turns** | `["Rend"]` |
| `['hp','shield']` (the alternative, **declined**) | `{ perTurn: 6, hit: 2, ehp: 27, soak: 30, fast: 5, slow: 5 }` — **≈5 turns** | `[]` |

The reasoning put to the developer and confirmed: a shield pool is bounded at 3 per Mech while the
projection assumes the action repeats without limit, so counting shield reports more throughput than
the board can deliver — and overstating is the one direction PROJ-06 forbids.

**`DAMAGE_KEYS` did not change.** It stands at `cats-vs-mechs.html:1998`:

```js
var DAMAGE_KEYS = Object.freeze(['hp']);
```

The rows that pin its figures — `[S09.2]`'s frozen-and-one-entry assertion at
`cats-vs-mechs.html:10106-10108`, and `[S09.8]`'s shipped-board control — are untouched and green.

**The correction stands too.** The phase context (D-14a) said the SHIPPED board would move from about 9
to about 5 under the wider reading and that fixtures should be updated. Measured under both readings in
the same run, it does **not** move either way: no shipped action carries a shield transformation, and
the pool `factionEhp` computes already includes shield. Nothing moved, and the plans pin that. This was
put to the developer as part of item 14 and approved with it.

## The Two Weakest Lines in This Record

**This section exists because the plan's acceptance criteria asked for two things a one-word approval
cannot produce, and a future reader must be able to see that.**

The plan required:

- *"Item 5's tone judgement is recorded in the developer's own words."*
- *"Item 10's actual behaviour is recorded verbatim, because it is untested code."*

The developer was given the full script for both items — item 5 with its framing (*"the only question in
this list that no test can answer, and the one the whole phase turns on"*), item 10 with its warning
(*"the Node stub has no close-request behaviour at all, so this is untested code"*) — and answered
**"approved"** for the whole set at once.

So:

- **Item 5 has no recorded prose.** The developer approved the item; they did not describe how the
  proposal reads. No tone judgement is quoted here because none was given, and inventing one would
  poison the single artifact this plan exists to produce.
- **Item 10 has no verbatim behaviour.** The developer approved the item, which under the resume-signal
  means the described behaviour held — Escape in a field reverts and leaves the dialog open, Escape with
  nothing focused closes it. But that is the *script's* description carried forward by approval, not an
  observation transcribed from the browser. Item 10 covers code the Node stub cannot reach at all, which
  is precisely why the plan asked for it verbatim.

Both criteria were satisfied by **blanket approval rather than by recorded prose**. That is not a
failure — the gate was put to a person, the person operated the artifact and approved it, and the plan's
own resume-signal defines "approved" as exactly this outcome. It is simply the truthful shape of the
record, and these two lines are the weakest in it. If the phase verifier or a later reader wants item 5's
tone judgement or item 10's close-request behaviour in the developer's own words, they must ask for them
again; they do not exist yet.
