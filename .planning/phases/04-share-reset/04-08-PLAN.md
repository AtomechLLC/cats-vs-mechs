<objective>
Put the phase in front of a person, because its core mechanism does not exist in the harness at all.

Every prior phase ended in a blocking rehearsal. This one has to be larger, and the reason is
specific: **the clipboard is a fourth kind of unreachable.** Layer C cannot see a dialog that never
opens; the stub cannot fire a close request; a Node sandbox cannot lay out a page. But
`navigator.clipboard` is not merely unrendered or unmeasured — it is **absent from the runtime**, and
so is `history`'s real behaviour, so is a reload, and so is a second browser. Of the four tiers the
copy path ships, exactly one is reachable from CI, and it is the last one.

The ROADMAP's verification note demands a real matrix rather than a smoke test: two browsers, three
focus states, and a forced fallback. It is written out cell by cell below.

And a note from the last rehearsal's own record: **03.1-08 closed on a one-word blanket approval, and
its SUMMARY names that as the record's weakest line.** Two items there — a tone judgement and an
untested close-request behaviour — became approvals of a description rather than recorded prose. This
checkpoint needs per-item answers, and the acceptance criteria say so.

Purpose: close the LOW-confidence list item by item, run the matrix, and hand six decisions back.
Output: a rehearsal record, and at most one constant or one control changed.
</objective>

       action's cost, requirement and transformation? Check the emoji name specifically.

    **B. LOADING, REFUSING, RELOADING.**

    8. Delete the last dozen characters from a good code, paste it, press Load. **Read the message
       aloud.** Does it tell you something you could act on? Record it in your own words.

       paste a sentence of ordinary prose. You should get **four different messages** across items 8
       and 9. Are all four useful, or are any of them the same sentence wearing different clothes?
    10. With a build you care about on screen, reload the page. Is it still there? Bookmark it, close
        the tab, reopen the bookmark. Still there?
    11. Open a link somebody shared (use one you copied out of the address bar into a new window).
        Press Ctrl+Z **immediately**. Nothing should happen — the board you opened is the starting
        state and there is nothing behind it. Is that what happened?
    12. Now damage that link: delete a few characters from the middle of the code in the address bar
        and load it. The **shipped** board should appear, with a message panel naming what was wrong,
        a dismiss control, and a page that still works afterwards. Also: did you see the shipped board
        flash and get replaced in item 11? No automated check can see a flash.
    13. Put `#selftest` in the hash **next to** a build code, separated by a comma. Both should work:
        the developer report opens and the board is the shared one. Then press a stepper — is
        `#selftest` still in the hash afterwards?

    **C. THE BOARD, THE SURFACE, AND THE ROOM.**

    14. Do both dialogs fit your laptop viewport? Now make a deliberately large board — many units,
        several invented types with tallies, long names — and open Share. Does the code field scroll
        rather than overflow the dialog?
    15. Stand back from the largest screen available, ideally the actual workshop display. Is the
        character count legible? Does it read as a **fact about the code** rather than as an opinion
        about your build?
    16. Push the board until the count passes the warning threshold. Read the line that appears. Is it
        descriptive — a statement about the code's length — or does it feel like the tool judging what
        you made?
    17. Look at the topbar from the back of the room. Does the reset control read as **visually apart**
        from the non-destructive controls, without needing to read the words?
    18. Escape behaviour, on **both** dialogs: press Escape while the cursor is inside the paste field
        — the field should revert and the dialog should stay **open**. Press Escape again with nothing
        focused — now it should close. Report exactly what happened. The Node stub has no
        close-request behaviour at all, so this is untested code in both surfaces.
    19. Press the reset control. Read the confirmation. Cancel — nothing should change. Press it again
        and confirm. Then press Ctrl+Z. **Does that feel like recovery?** The measurement says the
        board comes back byte-for-byte; whether a person believes it in the moment has never been
        rehearsed.
    20. The emoji name from item 7 — does it render correctly on the receiving side, in the picker, on
        the board, and in the share field's code?

    **D. SIX DECISIONS MADE ON YOUR BEHALF. Each is small now and expensive after Phase 5.**

    21. **One share dialog with two panes (D-21).** This was taken by the orchestrator rather than
        answered by you, and its own note says splitting is a cheap deviation if the panes fight.
        Having used it: do share and load belong in one surface, or should they be two?
    22. **Reset got its own dialog rather than a third pane.** The reasoning: a confirmation is a
        different act with a different opener, SHARE criterion 4 wants it visually apart, and Phase 5
        will want reset on the topbar beside share. Cost: about five extra shell ids and one more
        harvest root. **Confirm, or fold it into the share surface.**
    23. **The address-bar mirror is written with `history.replaceState` rather than by assigning the
        hash.** The reason: assigning pushes a history entry per commit, so your Back button would
        walk your whole edit history and the address bar would start behaving like a second undo
        stack — which SHARE-05 says never to present. **Confirm, or say you want Back to walk the
        edits.**
    24. **A build loaded from a link creates no undo entry (D-20), and the mechanism is a second
        named writer in the state section** — guarded so it can run once, and only before any commit
        has happened. You settled the behaviour; the mechanism was chosen by the plan, because the
        only alternative that keeps the single-writer rule was to commit normally and accept one
        entry. **Confirm the mechanism, or say you would rather have the entry.**
    25. **The over-budget threshold is 1,800 characters (D-18), and the line reads as recorded in
        plan 04-05's SUMMARY.** For scale: your realistic board is around 295 characters, a fully
        authored 24-versus-24 is around 675, and only a deliberately adversarial board reaches ~3,000.
        **Confirm the threshold and the wording, or move either.**
    26. **The 2,000-character message limit is the one number in this phase that is not measured.** It
        came from two mutually consistent secondary sources rather than from a first-party document,
        and it is the basis for item 25's threshold. Nothing else in the design depends on it. **Do
        you want it verified before the workshop, or is the threshold copy fine as it stands?**
