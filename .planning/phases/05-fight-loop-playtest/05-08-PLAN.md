<objective>
Step 3 and step 4 of the developer's own round loop: *"The previous state of the board moves up into a
history, so earlier rounds stay on screen"*, and *"the new state shows what changed since the previous
round — the student reads the effect of their own declaration without reconstructing it."*

**There is no append-only list render anywhere in this artifact.** Measured: every list surface in the
file is `replaceChildren()` and rebuild, or build-once-write-every-frame. The one structure in the
file that only grows — the undo stack — is capped at 30 and never rendered. So this region's growth
policy and its render tier are decisions with no precedent, and the nearest analog is `syncRow`, whose
rules are about token nodes inside one row rather than rows inside a region but whose **reason** is
exactly this project's:

> *"RULE 2 — grow and shrink by DELTA only. Node identity is the animation contract: 10 to 11 keeps
> all ten original nodes and appends exactly one. RULE 3 — never re-append, never reorder, never
> `row.append(...allNodes)` … removal from the document cancels that element's CSS animations so
> re-insertion replays them from the beginning."*

And its closing sentence, which is why this matters more here than anywhere: *"Every token replaying
its pop on a game-feel course artifact is a correctness bug, not a polish item."* A ledger that
rebuilt itself on every Advance would replay every past round's animations at once, on the artifact
for a game-feel course.

The cost is not the concern. Measured in Chrome 151 from `file://`: one more past-board row costs a
flat **1.9–2.1 ms all the way to 60 rounds and 26,132 nodes**, and `App.render.sync()` **does not move
at all** with a 50-round ledger on the page. The ledger is inert DOM outside the keyed reconcile. The
real constraints are node identity, `data-k` uniqueness, and readability.

Purpose: the past on screen, and the present readable against it. Output: `[S06.8]`, `[C14.2]`.
</objective>
