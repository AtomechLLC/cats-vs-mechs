**The fight becomes a full tab on the main screen, and the viewport problem dissolves structurally
rather than by turning a dial down.**

D-27, in the developer's own words: *"I don't like the pop up presentation for actions. I would
prefer having a full tab on the main screen."* This plan builds the tab and nothing else. The
declaration grid that fills it is plan 05-14's.

Four plans in a row each set a height dial against a page the next plan then changed, and the
orchestrator's out-of-sequence viewport fix bought 236px of headroom at 1080 and 38px at 768 by
laying `#fightbar` and `#ledger` side by side. **That arithmetic stops mattering the moment the
fight and the board are never on screen at the same time.** This is the structural answer the dial
sweep was standing in for.

What the switch may NOT take away is written into two requirements by name. PROJ-05: *"the
projection stays visible during the fight, so the student sees it at the moment the fight
contradicts it."* REF-03: *"reference material is readable without leaving the fight view."* So
`#strip` and `#refband` sit OUTSIDE both sides of the switch and are hidden by neither. That is
D-27's own interpretation call, and it is the one thing this plan must not get wrong.
