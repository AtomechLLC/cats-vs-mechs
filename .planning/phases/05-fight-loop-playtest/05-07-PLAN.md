<objective>
The surface the developer asked for, in the developer's own words: *"a simple input at the top where
you click actions to set the actions/moves for the current round."*

Its only ancestor in this file is `[S06.5]`'s proposal pane, and that pane's banner says out loud that
**it** had no precedent either: *"THIS IS THE ONE SURFACE IN THIS FILE WITH NO PRECEDENT. Nothing else
in this document stages a change for approval, so there was no shape here to copy … So the shape
chosen is a FORM."* And its closing sentence is addressed to this plan by name: *"AND IT WRITES
NOTHING. There is no App.ops call anywhere in this block or in the handlers that drive it. A declared
action lands on Advance, in Phase 5."*

So this is the proposal pane's sibling, one requirement later. It differs in exactly two ways and both
matter. It **does** write — a declaration is state, because FIGHT-12's *"before anything resolves"*
means an intent must exist and be undoable and survive a repaint. And what it stages is not a change
to a number but a **statement of intent for a round**, which is why it lives in `state.fight.decl`
rather than on the DOM, and why plan 05-03 wrote that argument into the file.

This plan also carries FIGHT-09, which is the requirement most likely to be quietly failed: *whose
turn it is and what remains to spend are unambiguous from across the room.* Simultaneous declaration
removes "whose turn". The readable facts are the round number and both pools, and *from across the
room* is a claim about a projector that only a rehearsal settles.

Purpose: what both sides are about to do, and what it costs against the board's real numbers.
Output: `[S06.7]`, `[C14.1]`, and not one press.
</objective>

**It disables nothing.** `[S06.5]`'s banner is the rule and check 71c is the mechanism: *"it never
disables a control on the grounds that a cost is unaffordable or a requirement unmet. The report is a
statement about what the BOARD holds; it is not a ruling on what the STUDENT may do."* Thirty-three
controls are compared across a board driven to zero action points, and probe V in 03.1-07 proved that
check fires.

D-23 is that rule applied to this surface: **a declared cost that exceeds the pool is reported, never
prevented.** An Advance control disabled because a side cannot afford its declaration reddens the same
class of row. If a control in this region is ever disabled, it is because the *tool* cannot do
something to itself — `setShareEnabled` and the picker's Remove are the two shipped examples — and
never because the *student* should not.

**It decides nothing else either.** The performer chooser lists the acting side's units and the chooser
for what an action points at lists **every** unit on the board, both factions, for 03.1-07's recorded
reason. This surface does not know what a counter is, does not read `REFERENCE.beats`, and shows no
figure that compares the two sides against each other or against a threshold — D-13 and PROJ-06 both.

And **nothing here is a verdict.** Plan 05-01 widened the word lists and pointed Layer C at this
region; the gate is a floor. `contested`, `even`, `close` and `one-sided` all pass it and none of them
ships.
